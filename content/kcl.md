## Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL)

**KCL:** The total current entering a node equals the total current leaving the node.

In other words, charge does not disappear or build up at a node.

$$
\sum I_{in} = \sum I_{out}
$$

or

$$
\sum I = 0
$$

where currents entering can be positive and currents leaving can be negative.

### Example

If 3 currents meet at one node:

$$
I_1 = I_2 + I_3
$$

This means the current coming into the node splits into two paths.

### When to use KCL

Use KCL when analyzing **nodes** in a circuit.

KCL is the basis of **nodal analysis**.

---

## Quick Comparison

| Law | Main Idea | Used For |
|---|---|---|
| KCL | Currents at a node sum to zero | Node analysis |
| KVL | Voltages around a loop sum to zero | Loop/mesh analysis |
