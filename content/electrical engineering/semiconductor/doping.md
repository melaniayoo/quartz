---
title: Doping
---

Doping is the process of adding a small amount of impurity atoms to a pure [[Semiconductor]].

It changes the number of mobile electrons or holes and increases conductivity.

Doping creates two main semiconductor types:

```text
Semiconductor
├── N-type → electrons are majority carriers
└── P-type → holes are majority carriers
```

# N-Type Semiconductor

N-type silicon is created by adding atoms with five valence electrons, such as:

* phosphorus
* arsenic

Silicon has four valence electrons.

Four dopant electrons form bonds with nearby silicon atoms, leaving one extra electron that can move through the material.

```text
Phosphorus added to silicon
            ↓
Four electrons form bonds
            ↓
One extra mobile electron
```

Therefore:

* majority carriers: electrons
* minority carriers: holes

The material is still electrically neutral overall.

# P-Type Semiconductor

P-type silicon is created by adding atoms with three valence electrons, such as:

* boron
* gallium

Because one bond is incomplete, a **hole** is created.

```text
Boron added to silicon
          ↓
One incomplete bond
          ↓
A hole is created
```

Nearby electrons can move into the hole, making the hole appear to move through the material.

Therefore:

* majority carriers: holes
* minority carriers: electrons

The material is still electrically neutral overall.

# N-Type vs. P-Type

| N-Type                          | P-Type                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Extra electrons                 | Missing electrons create holes |
| Electrons are majority carriers | Holes are majority carriers    |
| Uses donor atoms                | Uses acceptor atoms            |
| Common dopant: phosphorus       | Common dopant: boron           |

# Why Doping Is Important

Doping makes it possible to create:

* [[Diode]]
* [[Transistor]]
* [[MOSFET]]
* LEDs
* solar cells
* integrated circuits

# Key Idea

Doping controls semiconductor conductivity by changing the number and type of charge carriers.

N-type material mainly conducts using electrons, while P-type material mainly conducts using holes.
