## Resistivity

Resistivity describes how strongly a material opposes current.
Resistance also depends on the sample’s dimensions.

$$
R = \rho \frac{L}{A}
\qquad
\rho = R \frac{A}{L}
$$

- $L$: length along current flow.
- $A$: cross-sectional area perpendicular to current flow.
- Longer sample → higher resistance.
- Larger cross-sectional area → lower resistance.

| Material | Resistivity range (Ω·cm) |
| --- | --- |
| Conductor | 10⁻⁶–10⁻⁴ |
| Semiconductor | 10⁻⁴–10⁸ |
| Insulator | 10¹⁰–10¹⁸ |

Semiconductor electrical properties can be controlled through:
- **Doping:** adding impurities.
- **Illumination:** light can generate charge carriers.
- **Electric fields:** influence carrier motion and distribution.

## Semiconductor Materials

- **Elemental:** one element, e.g. Si and Ge (group IV).
- **Compound:** multiple elements, e.g. GaAs and InP (III–V).
- Si is widely used in diodes and transistors.
- Suitable compound semiconductors are used in LEDs and lasers.

## Types of Solids

| Type | Atomic ordering |
| --- | --- |
| Single-crystalline | Long-range order throughout the crystal |
| Polycrystalline | Ordered grains with different orientations, separated by grain boundaries |
| Amorphous | No long-range periodic order |

## Crystal Defects

- **Vacancy:** missing atom at a normal lattice site.
- **Interstitial:** extra atom between normal lattice sites.
- **Dislocation:** line defect disrupting the crystal arrangement.
  - The slide shows an edge dislocation: an extra half-plane of atoms terminates inside the crystal.

Crystal defects affect electronic properties and carrier motion.

> [!tip] Remember
> Vacancies and interstitials are **point defects**.
> Dislocations are **line defects**.
> Polycrystalline solids still have order within each grain.
